02|silence|Nobody calls. There is no alert, because an expiry date is not an event, it is just a field that quietly becomes the past. The next thing that happens is an inspector at the site, a stop work order, and a re-file that costs weeks. The failure mode of this entire trade is silence.

05|prose|The model is not here to decide any of that. Dates are arithmetic. Here is the part that is not. Of five hundred and eighty one violations the city still lists as active with a comment typed into a free text field, a keyword list correctly closes five hundred and seventy four. It leaves seven. Paid invoice. Challenge approved. Every one of those is a violation already settled, where acting would mean paying twice. Seven is a small number and it is the only number worth a model.
06|question|And when the arithmetic is sound but a fact is missing, Lapse stops and asks one question. This permit lapsed a hundred and eight days ago. The city waives the unpermitted work penalty only if nothing was done on the site after the date passed, and no dataset on earth holds that fact. So it asks, once, and shows every check it already ran to get there.
07|draft|When the rulebook does name the filing, the response is written, with the rule it rests on and the record it came from. And then it stops. Sending something on a contractor's behalf is not the agent's call to make, so it waits behind one button.
08|veto|That is not a prompt asking it nicely. A hook cancels the send unless the engine returned a filing verdict, the item is still open, and a draft exists for that exact case. Delete the hook and the suite goes red, because a test approves a case first and then calls send on a job the city already signed off. Put it back, and a hundred and sixty two tests pass.
