02|seven|A hundred and sixty five thousand violations are active. Five hundred and eighty one carry a hand typed disposition. A keyword list gets five hundred and seventy four of them right. It misses seven. That is the entire job of the language model in this system. Seven sentences.
03|datasets|Everything else is arithmetic on dates, and it runs on three city datasets that are keyed differently on purpose. Permits by business name. Job filings by job number. Violations by building. Nothing in the city's data answers the only question a contractor has: what do I owe this month.
04|cascade|So we asked it. Six hundred and five work permits went past their expiry date in the last thirty days. Collapse the renewal sequences and five hundred and forty two are real. Ask whether the city ever signed the job off, and four hundred and sixty three of them were still open work.
05|absence|That is the shape of it. The city publishes everything. The contractor finds out when it is already late. There is nothing in between, and that gap is the entire product.
06|engine|So the deterministic engine makes the call, on dates, and no prompt reaches it. The model sits underneath and reads the seven sentences arithmetic cannot. It decides nothing.
07|pass|On a real plumbing contractor's real public filings: two hundred and thirty five items screened. Sixteen with a filing due and a draft already written. Forty needing one question answered. And a hundred and seventy nine held completely.
08|holds|Every held item names the check that held it. Eighty seven were the building owner's obligation and not this contractor's. Nine sit on jobs the city already signed off. Three were renewed by a later sequence. Those are the alarms a naive join on building and date would have raised, and every one of them would have been wrong.
09|question|And when the arithmetic is sound but a fact is missing, it stops and asks one question. This permit lapsed months ago. The city waives the penalty only if nothing was done on site after the date passed, and no dataset holds that fact. So it asks, once, and puts no send button on the screen.
10|console|What the contractor opens is a queue, not a dashboard. Thirty three already late, three due this week, eight due this month. And the worst of them named outright: one thousand two hundred and eight days late, at forty three oh one seventh Avenue, Brooklyn.
11|draft|The response is already written, citing the rule it answers. It is not sent. Filing in a licensed contractor's name is not something software gets to decide.
12|gate|So it passes a hook that cancels the filing unless the engine returned a file verdict, and a human in the loop gate that waits for the contractor.
13|veto|And that is not theatre. In production, unrehearsed, a pass tried to file one twice. The hook stopped it. Case already filed, it will not be sent twice.
14|unattended|EventBridge Scheduler runs the whole thing daily against a Lambda, and every pass writes what it screened, what it held, and what it filed.
15|close|A permit does not fail loudly. It expires, on a date nobody announces. Lapse reads the date, writes the response, and asks once.
